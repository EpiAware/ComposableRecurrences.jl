Shapes and couplings ​

This page explains which slot takes which shape, how wrappers nest, and how a coupling mixes series. The shapes and couplings tables list the names, and each wrapper's docstring gives its axes.

julia
using ComposableRecurrences
using LinearAlgebra: I

Slots ​

A slot is a place an operator reads a coefficient from: a kernel, a coupling or a modifier parameter. Data (inputs, history and outputs) are not slots, and are always series × time. Each slot accepts its own set of wrappers.

Primary indexing is for kernels only, as the time-varying tutorial explains.

Nesting ​

Wrappers nest from the outside in: time, then strata, then the slot's own axes. So TimeVarying(PerStratum(G)) is an S × L × T kernel and TimeVarying(Pairwise(A)) is S × S × L × T. A Derived or Recent parameter sits inside arithmetic, as in κ * Recent(7), and its arguments may be any other parameter.

Every modifier reads a parameter through param, which takes every form. So a new wrapper or parameter form works with every modifier that reads through param, with no change to the modifier. The gradient mirror gives the matching rule for gradients.

Where the coupling acts ​

The coupling mixes the series after the kernel and before the gain. It has no lag of its own: it mixes each series' kernel-weighted past at the current step. For series, before the modifiers, a step computes

This order sets what a per-series coefficient means.

  • A PerStratum kernel belongs to the series sending, since the kernel weights each series' own past.

  • A gain of S rows belongs to the series receiving, since it multiplies the mixed value.

  • A row of the coupling is the series receiving, and a column the series sending.

So a sender's transmissibility goes in the kernel or the columns of the coupling, and a receiver's susceptibility in the gain or the rows. Scaling by a transmissibility gives the same step as scaling by .

julia
k = [0.4, 0.4, 0.2]
C = [0.8 0.3; 0.2 0.7]
gain = fill(1.1, 2, 8)
h = ones(2, 3)
σ = [1.0, 0.5]                  # transmissibility of each sender
by_kernel = Recurrence(PerStratum(σ .* k'); coupling = C)(gain; history = h)
by_columns = Recurrence(k; coupling = C .* σ')(gain; history = h)
by_kernel ≈ by_columns
true

Coupling or pairwise kernel ​

A coupling with a shared kernel assumes every pair of series has the same lag shape, scaled by the coupling. A Pairwise kernel gives each pair its own lag shape, so it does the coupling's job and the coupling stays I:

where weights series in series at lag . When every pair shares the shape,  , the two give the same values, and the coupling is cheaper.

julia
A = reshape(C, 2, 2, 1) .* reshape(k, 1, 1, :)
pairwise = Recurrence(Pairwise(A))(gain; history = h)
coupled = Recurrence(k; coupling = C)(gain; history = h)
pairwise ≈ coupled
true

Use a coupling when one lag shape fits, Routes for a few shapes, and Pairwise when the shape differs by pair.
